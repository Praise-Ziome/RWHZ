    Before you apply

    Short guides to help you avoid scams and stand out to international employers.

    How to spot a fake remote job
    • You're asked to pay. Application, training, software or equipment fees are a red flag.
    • Cheque or money-transfer requests. Anyone asking you to forward part of a payment is running a scam.
    • No real company trail. Hiring only through chat apps, with no company email, website or LinkedIn page.
    • Pay that's too good. High pay for easy work and no interview rarely turns out to be real.
    • Check first. Search the company name with the word "scam" before sharing documents or bank details.
    CV tips for remote roles
    • Keep it to one or two pages and tailor the top third to the job you're applying for.
    • Lead with results. "Cut load time by 40%" beats "responsible for performance".
    • Match the tools named in the listing, in your own words and only if you've used them.
    • Link your work: portfolio, GitHub or LinkedIn, so reviewers can check it quickly.
    • State your time zone and the hours you can overlap with the team.
    Remote interview tips
    • Plan for power and data cuts. Charge devices, keep a mobile hotspot ready and tell the interviewer your backup plan.
    • Test camera, microphone and the meeting link at least 15 minutes early.
    • Prepare two or three examples of remote or independent work you've done.
    • Ask how the team communicates: tools, meeting times and how work gets reviewed.
    • Follow up within a day with a short thank-you email.
